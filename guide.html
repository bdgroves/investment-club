How to Fill Out a Trade Journal

You don't need to be a finance expert to submit a great idea. The tool does the number-crunching; you bring the thinking. Here's what every field means, in plain English.

The big idea: a submission has two halves. The numbers (price, ratios) fill in automatically. The thinking (why you like it) is the part only you can write — and it's the part the club actually cares about. Nobody's grading you; you're starting a conversation.

1The numbers — these fill themselves in

Just type the ticker symbol (like AAPL for Apple or Z for Zillow) and click Auto-fill. These populate on their own — here's what they mean, so you know what you're looking at:

Share value — today's price for one share.
1-year high / low — the highest and lowest price over the past year. Tells you where today's price sits in that range.
Market capitalization — the company's total size. "Large" means a household-name giant; "Small" means a smaller, often riskier company.
Beta — how jumpy the stock is compared to the market. 1.0 moves right along with the market; above 1 swings more; below 1 is steadier.
P/E ratio — price-to-earnings. How expensive the stock is compared to the profit it makes. A high number means investors are paying up for big expectations.
Earnings per share (EPS) — the company's profit divided by its number of shares. Negative means it isn't profitable yet.
Price / revenue per share — another way to gauge how pricey a stock is — this time against sales instead of profit.
Dividend per share — the cash the company pays you per share each year, if any. Many growth companies pay nothing (that's fine).
Growth or income — are you buying it hoping the price climbs (growth), or mainly for the dividend checks (income)?

2The thinking — this part is yours

This is the heart of it. Write like you're explaining the idea to a friend over coffee. A few honest sentences beat a wall of jargon.

Core thesis — in plain words: what does the company do, and why will it do well? One or two sentences.
Good: "Costco keeps winning loyal members who renew every year, and membership fees alone are a steady, growing profit machine."   Too vague: "Good company, everyone shops there."
Competitive moat — the company's edge that keeps competitors from catching up. Think brand, size, patents, a network of users, or the hassle of switching away.
Example: "Once a company runs its whole business on their software, ripping it out is painful — so customers stay for years."
Why now — why buy it now rather than later? A catalyst, a recent change, or good timing.
Example: "They just launched in a huge new market, and the stock hasn't caught up to that yet."
Pros — the bull case. The main reasons this could work out well.
Cons — the bear case. What could go wrong, or what worries you. (Naming the risks makes a submission stronger, not weaker — it shows you thought it through.)
Think through every outcome — your game plan. If it goes up, do you buy more or take profits? If it drops, do you hold or sell? What would change your mind?
Don't overthink it. You won't have every answer, and that's completely okay — a submission is the start of a club discussion, not a final exam. Fill in what you can, be honest about what you're unsure of, and let the group help with the rest. Every member's perspective makes the club smarter.

3Where to do it

Open the club website (use the link and password shared by the club). Click Submit to add a trade, or Review Desk to see everyone's submissions and the AI's discussion summary. That summary doesn't tell the club what to buy — it just lays out the ideas and the questions worth talking about, so the meeting starts warm.
